In my desktop use, laser SLA gave a modest improvement in surface finish over masked SLA; otherwise it behaved much the same, only slower. I first used it through a service vendor, before in-house print farms were common.

Its real value is speed of learning. An overnight print lets you screen dimensional fit, use case, and user experience without waiting weeks for a quality machine shop, which makes it ideal for iterating through design concepts.

The smooth finish can also mislead. I printed manifolds for small-diameter tubing that looked excellent, and that appearance gave a false sense of quality: the tubing held better on a textured surface than on the polished one. Judge resin parts by function, not appearance.
