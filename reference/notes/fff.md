I have used FFF for engineering prototypes, production manufacturing, and hobby work. It wins on less complex parts with few curves and modest resolution. For prototyping it competes with resin printing and almost always beats a machine shop, unless the part needs specific features such as bearing surfaces. For an early startup, a good printer under $1,000 makes excellent parts and saves the time lost to outsourcing.

Layer failures happen. Most fixes are more perimeters, more infill, and an enclosure, depending on the material. Reorienting the part helps only occasionally, and only when you can rotate it a full 90 degrees.

PETG is my default; PLA when I need speed or am out of PETG. ABS and TPU work well for specific applications.

Design matters more than you think: design the part correctly, then design the slicer file correctly. Both count far more here than with resin.
